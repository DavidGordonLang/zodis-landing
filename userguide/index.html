The main bit

The guided course

Beta 3 has a five-section course that takes you from greetings and introductions through numbers, food and drink, directions, places and everyday conversation.

I’ve tried to keep the lessons short and practical. The idea is not to throw a grammar textbook at you — it is to teach a useful bit of Lithuanian, make sure you understand it, then get you using it.

Not just multiple choice

What you’ll do in lessons

There’s a mixture of activities because I found that recognising an answer and actually being able to say it are very different things.

  • Learn: new words, phrases and useful word changes are introduced before you’re tested on them.
  • Meaning choices: work out what the Lithuanian actually means.
  • Build the phrase: put a Lithuanian sentence together yourself.
  • Match the pairs: quick recall of words and meanings.
  • Say it out loud: hear it, then try saying it yourself.
  • Lesson scenarios: use what you’ve learned in a short real-life conversation.

Žodis also tries not to be overly strict. If what you say works but is not the nicest answer for that moment, it can let you carry on and show you the better version rather than simply shouting “wrong”.

When you just need a phrase

Home: translation with context

The course is the learning path, but sometimes you just need to know how to say something now. That’s what Home is for.

  • Type what you want to say or use the mic where available.
  • Tell Žodis who you’re speaking to and whether the tone is friendly or polite.
  • Choose English-style phonetics or IPA.
  • Listen to the Lithuanian and save anything useful.
Why all the context controls? Because Lithuanian changes depending on who is speaking, who you’re talking to and how formal the situation is. I’d rather give you the right version than pretend one translation fits everything.

The useful stuff you want to keep

Your Library

This is one of the parts I use most myself. Save something to the Library and Žodis does more than file away the translation — it builds it out into something you can actually come back to and learn from.

  • Anything you save is enriched with advanced notes and usage context.
  • Useful alternative Lithuanian phrases are added too when there are natural alternatives worth knowing.
  • You can search, sort, edit and replay anything you’ve saved.
  • Tap individual Lithuanian words for audio where word playback is available.
  • At course milestones, you choose which vocabulary is worth keeping instead of having everything dumped into your Library automatically.

There’s also an optional Daily Recall that can show you one saved phrase when you open the app. It’s deliberately small — just a quick nudge to keep useful language in your head.

The bit I personally need constantly

Audio, slow playback and speaking

Lithuanian spelling is fairly consistent, but that didn’t mean I always knew what a phrase was supposed to sound like. So audio is built into pretty much everything.

  • Play full phrases at normal speed.
  • Use slow playback when your ear needs a bit more time.
  • Tap individual words in supported parts of the app.
  • Switch between English-style phonetics and IPA.
  • In speaking exercises, listen first and then try the phrase yourself.

The speaking checker keeps its feedback simple. You shouldn’t need to understand speech-recognition diagnostics just to practise a sentence.

Putting it into a conversation

Two kinds of Scenarios

The word “Scenario” crops up in two places, so this is worth explaining.

Lesson scenarios

Authored conversations inside the course — cafés, directions, shopping, asking for help and so on. They’re there to make you use the language you’ve just learned.

Your own Scenarios

Personal phrase collections you create yourself — maybe visiting family, ordering your usual coffee, an appointment or an upcoming trip.

In lesson scenarios, some choices test whether you understood the Lithuanian, while others ask you to actually produce a reply. Help is usually staged, so you get a clue before being handed the full English answer.

Pick up where you left off

Progress, Review and Settings

If you leave a lesson halfway through, Žodis remembers where you were and keeps the mistakes you’d already made as part of that attempt. Completed lessons stay completed, and Review lets you go back through them without messing up your progress.

Settings is where you can tweak the bits that are personal to you: your profile, voice, phonetics, Daily Recall, the in-app guide and Library/Scenario sync.

Library sync: Upload, Download and Merge are deliberately separate actions. If both your device and cloud have useful changes, Merge is normally the safest option. Course progress is handled separately from Library sync.

The guided course, translation, Library, lesson scenarios, audio and progress are all live. A couple of extra Training areas are intentionally still marked Coming soon — they are not missing pieces of the current course.
